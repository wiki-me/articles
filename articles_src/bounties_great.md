# Why bounties can be great for open source projects 

[software bounties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_bounty) basically mean people post how much they are willing to pay to have a feature or bug fixed worked on. then someone finds it and if it pays enough he implements it.

The biggest advantage of bounties is that they give users a way to incentivize directly, people usually say to a developer “you are not your users” and developers might prioritized the wrong thing. a good bounty platform can create a market for contributions that will enable to estimate their worth. it also gives a bigger incentive to give money. Real world money is used as a indication of priority which is better then something like github thumbs up because [incentives can reduce biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debiasing) . it is not the ultimate solution to funding open source projects (that doesn’t exist) but as part of a funding plan it can be very useful in the highly competitive world of software development.

You can think of bounties like a auction, the group of people that will offer the most money will get it's work done.

Sure success of bounties seems limited ( [JHipster](https://blog.opencollective.com/jhipsters-bounty-system-and-how-it-saved-the-project/) is a notable exception) , but i feel like it was never tried in the most skillful manner, every project i saw that tried to use it had problems  like not having a way to sort by most funded bountries  and issues with [conversation rate](https://www.optimizely.com/optimization-glossary/conversion-rate/) where too many users don't know about that option, try linking to it on the issue template of a big open source project (and other proper linking like the github "funding" button) and see what happens.

thunderbird spike in revenue [seems](https://blog.thunderbird.net/2023/05/thunderbird-is-thriving-our-2022-financial-report/) to be due to a higher conversation ratio. so i think it is important to take that under consideration.
